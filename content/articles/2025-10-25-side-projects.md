# My one rule for side projects

Ah, side projects. The one place where you get to build things purely for the joy of it. Where you can explore new tech and new ideas without answering to anyone but yourself.

But it's kind of easy to ruin a side project, as I've learned the hard way. Twice. Don't use the same tech stack as your day job!

## The rise and fall of my first side project

Back in 2001, my passion project was a music fan site. I loved working on this site. I spent countless hours building my own PHP-based CMS for it, and enjoyed every line of code, every new feature. It was pure fun.

In 2005 I got a new day job, and my professional life suddenly revolved around building websites with TYPO3, a powerful and notoriously complex open-source PHP CMS. After about a year, feeling like a TYPO3 guru, I had an idea: why not migrate my fan site over to TYPO3? It made perfect sense. I was working with it all day anyway.

Huge mistake.

After eight hours working with TYPO3, coming home to do the exact same thing felt like a second shift instead of a hobby. It wasn't joyful anymore, and it was really hard to stay motivated to keep working on this site. One day, without planning or warning, I simply pulled the plug.

I didn't start another user-facing side project for almost fifteen years.

## History repeats itself

Fast forward to 2020. The itch returned, and I started something new: [critical-notes.com](https://www.critical-notes.com). I started with Svelte and Firebase, eventually moving the stack to SvelteKit and Django by late 2021. My day job was pure iOS development, so this side project was a completely different kind of experience. I loved working on this site.

Then in 2023 I decided to [pivot professionally](/articles/2025/thoughts-on-apple/) and return to the open web. My new client's project involved a SvelteKit frontend and a Django backend. Yup, the exact same technologies powering Critical Notes. "Brilliant!" I thought. "I can use my side project knowledge at work, and vice versa."

I should've known better...

Sure enough, the motivation began to fade. After a full day immersed in SvelteKit and Django at work, the thought of spending my free time building new features for Critical Notes felt like, well, _work_. I struggled to find the energy to keep working on my side project.

## My advice

Absolutely have a side project. But please keep its tech stack different from the one you use at work, or you'll turn your side project into a second job. Let it be your escape, your playground, and enjoy yourself.
