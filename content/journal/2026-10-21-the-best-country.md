The score is made up of several categories:

* Language. Bonus points when the majority of people understand English or Dutch. A penalty against countries that don't use the Latin alphabet, since that makes everything so much harder.
* Annoyances. Small penalties for countries that drive on the left, don't use the metric system, or use Fahrenheit.
* Weather. A penalty for very warm countries, because I simply can't deal with it.
* Visa. How easy is it to move to this country, to live and work there long-term, without being afraid of being deported when you lose your job?
* Safety. A score based on the Numbeo Crime Index and the Global Peace Index. It accounts for crime and things like war, but also for how safe people feel when they're walking home in the dark.
* Quality of life. A score based on the Social Progress Index and the UN Human Development Index. It includes things like access to basic needs (including good internet), the quality of healthcare, and pollution.
* Happiness. A score based on the World Happiness Report.
* Disasters. Penalties for exposure to floods, earthquakes, tsunamis and hurricanes.
* Fireworks. Bonus points for countries where the general population can't buy and use fireworks. It's just horrible for people with pets, and for the environment.
* Politics. The most subjective one, with bonuses based on things like women's rights and gender equality, LGBTQ+ rights, freedom of press, a welfare state, sanctions against Israel, and ICC membership. And penalties for things like institutional racism, siding with Russia, authoritarian rule, lack of gun control, police brutality, right-wing government, anti-vaccine, and climate change denial.
* Nature. Bonus points for exceptional nature.
* Transport. Bonus points for good and affordable public transportation, good bicycle infrastructure, good roads, walkable cities.

Politics, quality of life, and safety are the three most important factors for me when looking at countries I'd want to live in, so the biggest points (as well as the biggest penalties) could be had in these categories, ranging from -5 to +5. Most other categories go from -2 to +2.

It didn't come as a surprise that the United States scored negative one point. I don't even want to go on a holiday there, let alone live there. Sorry.

According to this list I should move back to Iceland, move to Norway, or stay in the Netherlands. Not entirely unexpected, since Norway was already on my mind before making this list, but it's nice that the data backs it up.

I'm pretty sure I'll end up in Norway, Denmark, Austria, or Ireland. I loved my time in Iceland, but it's way too touristy and overcrowded now, so I don't see myself living there again. Denmark and Austria have the advantage of being within driving distance of my family and fiends in the Netherlands, while Norway has the most picturesque nature, Denmark the best bicycle story, and Ireland the best vibes. 

Switzerland would be preferable over Austria, but it has such a high cost of living that working as a remote freelancer becomes almost impossible; my Dutch clients wouldn't pay the sort of rates necessary to live in Switzerland. But let's say that I'd find a good job in a beautiful mountain village? I would move immediately. I lost my heart in Kandersteg and living there for a few years would be the dream.
